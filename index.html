US Flight Delays and Cancellations in January 2024

Author

Zhiyang Cheng

For this project I loaded all 547,271 US domestic flights from January 2024 into a MySQL database on AWS and answered the seven assignment questions with SQL. This page shows what I found. The queries and full results are on the SQL Questions page.

In January 2024, 20,389 flights (3.7%) were cancelled. Most of them came from a week of winter storms in the middle of the month and from the grounding of the Boeing 737 MAX 9.

Cancellations by day

Every cancelled flight has a reason code. Weather cancellations came in spikes. The worst day was January 15 with 2,962 cancellations, and January 12 to 16 alone had 46% of all cancellations in the month.

Carrier cancellations, the ones caused by the airline itself, look different. There were about 16 a day from January 1 to 5, then about 335 a day from January 6 to 27. On January 5 a door plug blew out of an Alaska Airlines 737 MAX 9 during a flight, and the FAA grounded these planes the next day. Alaska and United were the two US airlines flying this model. Alaska started flying them again on January 26 and United on January 27.

Cancellations by airline

Alaska cancelled 17.6% of its flights and United 8.0%. Every other airline was under 6%, and Delta was the lowest at 0.6%. Alaska and United also had 6,895 of the 7,736 carrier cancellations (89%). The data has no aircraft type column, so I can’t connect a single flight to the grounding, but the airlines and the dates match.

Delays by airline

Delays rank the airlines differently. American had the highest average departure delay at 25.5 minutes, but it cancelled only 1.6% of its flights. Alaska and United were in the middle at about 19 minutes.

Delays by time of day

Delays build up during the day, probably because a late plane also makes its next flight late. Only 9.9% of flights scheduled between 6 and 7 a.m. left 15 or more minutes late. For flights scheduled between 7 and 8 p.m. it was 32.1%.

Small airports

The airport with the highest average departure delay (Problem 4) is North Central West Virginia airport (CKB) at 86 minutes. But CKB had only 15 departures in January, so a few very late flights decide its average. Some answers in Problem 5 have the same problem.

Data and method

The data is the Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance table from the Bureau of Transportation Statistics for January 2024, with one row per flight. I created the schema FAA on AWS RDS (MySQL) and the fact table al_perf with the course script, and loaded the CSV with mysqlimport. I loaded the six lookup tables (airlines, airports, airport IDs, cancellation reasons, distance groups and weekdays) with mysqlimport too. These files have Windows line endings, so my first load merged every two rows into one until I added --lines-terminated-by='\r\n'.

mysqlimport saves empty numbers as 0. A cancelled flight that never left the gate has an empty departure time and delay, so its delay becomes 0 after loading. For the delay questions I only use flights that have a departure time (DepTime <> '').

The charts are made with Python (pandas and Plotly) from the query results, and the site is built with Quarto.
